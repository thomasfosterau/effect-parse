Counts and repetition bounds are validated when a parser is built, and a length read from the input is validated when it is read (#114, #121). `Token.take`, `Text.*.take`, `Bits.take` / `takeBig` / `pattern` and every combinator that takes a `Range` (`repeat`, `fold`, `repeatTill`, `separated`, `takeWhile`, `takeTill`, `takeUntil`, `takeUntilAny` and their `Text` and `Nom` forms) now throw a `RangeError` unless the count, `min` and `max` are non-negative integers (`max` may be `Infinity`) with `min <= max`; `escaped` / `takeEscaped` throw unless the control character is a character or its code. Before, a negative or fractional count moved the cursor backwards (`Parser.repeat(Binary.lengthTake(Binary.i8))` on the single byte `0xfe` never terminated and ran the process out of memory), and the JIT wrote a bound into the source it compiles as given, so a bound that was a string at runtime (one read from JSON without a `Number()`) ran as code. `Binary.lengthTake`, `lengthRepeat` and `lengthAndThen` now fail the parse at the length, with kind `Verify`, when it is negative, fractional, `NaN` or above `Number.MAX_SAFE_INTEGER`. The JIT also writes every number through one helper that refuses anything but a non-negative integer.
